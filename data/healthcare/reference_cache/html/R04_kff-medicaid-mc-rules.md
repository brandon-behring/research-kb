# What You Need to Know About Medicaid Managed Care, Amid the New Federal Rules

## Overview

Managed care represents the dominant delivery system for Medicaid across the United States. The federal government recently released updated rules strengthening access standards for these programs, establishing national maximum wait times for routine appointments.

## Key Statistics

**Enrollment and Market Share:**
- Approximately 3 out of 4 Medicaid enrollees receive care through managed care organizations
- Payments to these organizations account for over half of total Medicaid spending
- 42 states (including DC) use comprehensive managed care plans for at least some enrollees

**Market Concentration:**
Five publicly traded firms dominate the sector: Centene, UnitedHealth Group, Elevance (formerly Anthem), Molina, and CVS/Aetna, collectively representing half of managed care enrollment.

## New Federal Requirements

The updated rules require states to monitor and enforce key standards aimed at improving access to care. These include establishing "national maximum wait times for routine primary care, OB/GYN, and behavioral health appointments."

## Impact

Historically, states maintained broad discretion in defining and enforcing network adequacy standards. The new framework shifts responsibility toward federal oversight and standardized requirements that will be phased in over time, potentially creating significant implications for states, plans, and enrollees.
