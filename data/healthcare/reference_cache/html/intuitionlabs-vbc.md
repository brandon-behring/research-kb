# Value-Based Contracting in Pharma: Models & Challenges

## Executive Summary
VBC ties pharmaceutical reimbursement to real-world clinical/economic performance. Models include outcomes-based schemes, financial risk-sharing, indication-specific pricing, and subscription models. 58%+ of payers have at least one outcomes-based contract, but no systematic evidence yet demonstrates spending reduction or outcome improvement.

## Models
| Model Type | Key Characteristics | Primary Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Outcomes-based | Payment tied to patient-level clinical targets | Align reimbursement with outcomes |
| Financial Risk-Sharing | Capped spending, manufacturer covers excess | Budget predictability |
| Subscription/Netflix | Flat fee for unlimited access | Broad population treatment |
| Indication-Specific | Differential pricing by subgroup | Align price with expected value |
| Hybrid | Combines outcome + financial elements | Balanced risk/reward |

## Key Statistics
- U.S. pharma expenditures: $806B in 2024 (+10.2% YoY)
- Specialty medicines projected >55% of drug spending by 2028
- 30% of Americans skip medications due to cost
- McKinsey identified ~200 innovative arrangements globally since 1994, ~50 in U.S.

## Regulatory Milestones
- CMS finalized VBP rule (effective Jan 1, 2026): allows multiple best-price reporting
- CMS Cell and Gene Therapy Access Model (2025): 33 states + DC + Puerto Rico
- IRA Medicare negotiations: 10 drugs in 2026, 15 for 2027, 20/year from 2029

## Case Studies
- **J&J Velcade (2007, UK):** Reimbursement if <50% paraprotein reduction
- **Merck Januvia (2009, Cigna):** Rebates tied to HbA1c improvements
- **State HepC subscriptions:** LA ~$290M/5yr, WA ~$321M/4yr — both underperformed targets

## Key Barriers
- Contract complexity and high transaction costs
- Inadequate data infrastructure
- Patient mobility between insurers
- Measurement disagreements
- Regulatory uncertainty despite recent progress
