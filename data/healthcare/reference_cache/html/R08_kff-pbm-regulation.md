# What to Know About Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs) and Federal Efforts at Regulation

## Market Concentration

Three companies dominate: top 3 PBMs manage 79% of prescription drug claims for 270 million people (2023).

- CVS Caremark (34%)
- Express Scripts (23%)
- OptumRx (22%)
- Other PBMs (21%)

Each major PBM is vertically integrated with large health insurers and owns affiliated pharmacies.

## Problematic Business Practices

**Rebate arrangements:** PBMs negotiate rebates and may retain portions, incentivizing higher-priced drugs with larger rebates.

**Spread pricing:** PBMs pay pharmacies lower rates than charged to insurers, retaining the difference.

**Transparency gaps:** Financial contracts remain largely confidential.

## Recent Federal Actions (2026)

Congress enacted PBM reforms in February 2026 through H.R. 7148:

**Medicare Part D provisions:**
- Delinking PBM compensation from drug prices (effective Jan 1, 2028)
- Annual transparency/reporting requirements (July 1, 2028)
- Reasonable pharmacy access standards (Jan 1, 2029)

**Employer health plan oversight:**
- Detailed prescription drug utilization/spending data reporting
- 100% pass-through of rebates to ERISA-covered employer plans
- Expanded "covered service provider" definitions

## Budgetary Impact

CBO estimates $2.12B federal deficit reduction over 10 years:
- $444M from Part D delinking/transparency
- $1.865B from employer plan oversight
- $188M cost from pharmacy access provisions

## Related Policy Efforts

Trump administration proposed DOL rule requiring PBM compensation disclosure to self-insured plan fiduciaries. FTC maintains pending lawsuits against Caremark and Optum regarding insulin pricing.
