# HMO vs PPO vs POS vs EPO: What's the Difference?

## Overview

The type of managed care plan you choose significantly affects your healthcare costs, available benefits, and access to medical providers. Understanding the distinctions between Health Maintenance Organizations (HMOs), Preferred Provider Organizations (PPOs), Exclusive Provider Organizations (EPOs), and Point of Service (POS) plans is essential when selecting health coverage.

## What is Managed Care?

Managed care represents a medical delivery system designed to manage both quality and cost of medical services. These plans limit the number of available providers, whether patients must use a primary care physician, and whether out-of-network care receives coverage.

The vast majority of privately insured Americans participate in some form of managed care. Notably, "less than 1% of enrollees were in indemnity (non-managed-care) plans" among those with employer-sponsored insurance. All plans available through health insurance Marketplaces are managed care plans.

## Four Types of Managed Care

### HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)

HMO coverage generally requires using in-network providers except for emergencies. Most plans require selecting a primary care physician who serves as a "gatekeeper," meaning specialist referrals are typically necessary before coverage applies.

However, some modern HMOs labeled "open access" do not require PCP referrals to see specialists. As of 2023, approximately 53% of Marketplace plans were HMOs.

### PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)

PPO plans offer discounted rates through in-network providers while covering out-of-network care at higher out-of-pocket costs. Specialist referrals are generally not required. However, "policyholders who use a provider outside of the PPO plan's network typically pay more for the medical care" and face potential balance billing from out-of-network providers.

As of 2023, only 14% of Marketplace plans were PPOs.

### EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization)

EPO plans cover only in-network care except in emergencies. Unlike HMOs, EPOs do not require primary care physicians or specialist referrals, allowing direct access to network specialists. Since out-of-network care lacks coverage, patients pay full price for non-emergency out-of-network services.

Approximately 29% of Marketplace plans were EPOs as of 2023.

### POS (Point of Service)

POS plans typically require selecting a primary care physician and obtaining specialist referrals. These plans do cover out-of-network care after referral, but out-of-pocket expenses are substantially higher for out-of-network services. Out-of-network providers can balance bill policyholders unless No Surprises Act protections apply.

POS plans remain uncommon, representing only 4% of Marketplace plans as of 2023.

## Key Differences

### Access to Providers

All plan types maintain networks of contracted providers accepting negotiated prices. However, coverage rules vary significantly. While simple comparisons suggest HMOs and POS plans require referrals while PPOs and EPOs do not, reality proves more nuanced. "In reality, these distinctions tend to be blurry, and these types of plan management have evolved over time."

### Out-of-Network Care

EPO and HMO plans typically exclude non-emergency out-of-network care, while PPO and POS plans provide coverage at higher costs. The ACA caps in-network out-of-pocket costs at $10,600 for 2026, but "there are no limits on how high out-of-network costs can be, even if the plan provides out-of-network coverage."

### Geographic Availability

Plan availability varies by location. PPOs have become increasingly rare in individual markets, while "HMOs and EPOs have gained considerable market share over the last several years." For 2025 coverage across states using HealthCare.gov:

- 47% of plans were HMOs
- 30% of plans were EPOs
- 15% of plans were PPOs
- 7% of plans were POS

## Considerations When Selecting a Plan

1. **Understand out-of-network coverage details** including deductibles, out-of-pocket limits, and balance billing implications
2. **Compare provider networks actively** rather than assuming breadth; network size varies across all plan types
3. **Recognize individual market differences** from employer-sponsored plans regarding networks and managed care rules
4. **Verify referral requirements** for your specific plans, as requirements vary even within categories
5. **Understand prior authorization rules**, which differ across plans regardless of type

## Important Distinction: HDHP and HSA

High-deductible health plans (HDHPs) and health savings accounts (HSAs) are not managed care categories themselves. An HDHP may be structured as an HMO, PPO, EPO, or POS plan. The HDHP designation indicates IRS compliance for HSA eligibility, while the HMO/PPO/EPO/POS label describes the managed care approach.
