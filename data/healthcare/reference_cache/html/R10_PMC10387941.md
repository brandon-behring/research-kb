# Best Practices in Designing Preapproval Information Engagements for US Health Care Decision Makers

**Journal:** JMCP, March 2023, Vol 29, Issue 3
**DOI:** 10.18553/jmcp.2023.29.3.245 | **PMID:** 36840953
**Authors:** Sai Dodda, Breyanne Bannister, Tasmina Hydery, Claire Gorey, Sarah Dunlap, Lorie Mody (Xcenda, LLC)

## Key Findings

### Current State of PIE Usage
- 83% of respondents had experience with preapproval information formats
- 74% reported using PIE in formulary decision-making
- Manufacturers increasingly establish internal PIE guidance (nearly doubled 2018-2020)

### Preferred Content
HCDMs prioritize: product indication, clinical trial design/results, factual efficacy data, disease prevalence

### Timing Preferences
Communications should occur within 12 months of anticipated approval

### Therapeutic Areas of Interest
- Diabetes (30%), Oncology (30%), Atopic dermatitis (22%)

### Speaker Preferences for Webinars
- Clinical experts/specialists: 96%
- Peer HCDMs: 61%
- Health economists: 61%
- Manufacturer representatives: 13%

### Identified Gaps
- 45% of HCDMs noted gap between available and needed preapproval information
- Pricing remains contentious
- Confidentiality concerns and resource constraints limit participation

## Methodology
FormularyDecisions survey, 23 respondents (74% participation). PBMs (n=10), health plans (n=7), academic centers (n=3), IDNs/independent practices/self-funded employers (n=3).

## Recommendations
1. Tailor content to HCDM priorities — emphasize clinical evidence and budget impact over pricing
2. Provide clearer guidance and legislative safe harbors
3. Establish clear data requirements and timelines for engagement
